# Capability-Decomposition Method — Principal Adoption Record

**Date:** September 25, 2026  
**Status:** Principal-adopted decomposition methodology for the governed thin-slice decomposition phase  
**Decision:** Principal selected Option A: adopt the converged corrected method produced by independent Codex and Kiro adversarial reviews and ChatGPT reconciliation.  
**Repository state before decision persistence:** accepted `main` remotely verified at `68e3d848d305f762d684b5d35a9a6956d4c395f9`.  
**Scope:** methodology only. This record does **not** itself derive a capability inventory, select the thin-slice endpoint, resolve Product-policy gaps, design a capability, or authorize implementation.

## 1. Decision

Adopt a capability-first decomposition method with these governing corrections:

1. Freeze the operator-visible thin-slice outcome before capability derivation.
2. Derive capabilities from required Product behavior, authority/state transitions, and independent failure boundaries—not from technology layers, ontology enumeration, or projection of current code.
3. Treat research/discovery as evidence, not implementation authority. If the frozen outcome depends on behavior whose governing meaning remains unresolved, expose an authority prerequisite and block only the affected path rather than silently implementing it or automatically pretending the behavior is irrelevant.
4. Establish load-bearing cross-capability integration contracts before independent cluster designs freeze.
5. Maintain a walking end-to-end specimen throughout delivery; integration is a constraint on decomposition and implementation, not a final phase.
6. Reconcile only material unresolved Product meaning, authority, semantic identity, temporal/historical truth, safety/failure behavior, ratified architecture, thin-slice scope, or cross-capability invariants. Ordinary design and implementation choices remain local engineering judgment.
7. Require local cluster acceptance plus cumulative end-to-end acceptance.
8. Permit controlled adaptation when implementation evidence reveals a concrete contradiction; do not treat decomposition as falsely final and do not reopen settled boundaries for merely interesting or elegant observations.

## 2. Frozen thin-slice contract precedes derivation

Before deriving capabilities, establish the thin-slice outcome contract:

- operator problem;
- start state;
- observable operator-visible result;
- positive specimen(s);
- negative/adverse/refusal specimen(s);
- restart/replay expectation where applicable;
- explicit endpoint;
- explicit non-goals.

Capability derivation is not permitted to substitute for choosing this boundary.

## 3. Authority map

Before capability derivation, distinguish:

- ratified authority;
- unresolved Product policy;
- discovery/research evidence;
- implementation facts that may be reused but do not govern Product meaning.

Every load-bearing behavior must be traceable to sufficient authority. Discovery/research does not become Product policy merely because it appears useful.

However, discovery/research is **not automatically DEFER**. If a frozen required outcome genuinely depends on unresolved meaning found only in discovery/research, decomposition must surface the missing authority as an explicit prerequisite. Only the affected behavior is blocked pending reconciliation.

## 4. Responsibility landscape

Derive candidate capabilities/clusters from:

- operator-visible behavior;
- authoritative ownership;
- durable lifecycle or temporal behavior;
- independently meaningful failure/safety boundaries;
- required state transitions;
- independently testable empirical questions where relevant.

Do not promote a semantic noun to a capability merely because it exists in the ontology.

Do not allow existing implementation boundaries to define Product capability boundaries.

For each candidate record:

- Product behavior owned;
- authority/state owner;
- REQUIRED / ENABLING / DEFER disposition;
- SPLIT / CLUSTER disposition;
- explicit non-responsibilities;
- thin-slice specimen(s) enabled;
- relevant non-goals excluded.

## 5. REQUIRED / ENABLING / DEFER

### REQUIRED

Without it, at least one frozen positive, negative, refusal, restart, replay, audit, or operator-visible specimen cannot succeed truthfully.

### ENABLING

The outcome might theoretically exist without it, but it is the smallest coherent shared mechanism necessary to prevent a concrete failure such as:

- competing authority;
- unreplayable history;
- unsafe integration;
- duplicated load-bearing semantics;
- inability to observe/accept the slice.

An ENABLING capability must name the failure it prevents.

### DEFER

Removing it leaves the frozen specimens safe, reproducible, observable, and informative. Its value is future breadth, scale, convenience, polish, reuse, or an unresolved future Product possibility.

A candidate that cannot name a frozen specimen or demonstrated integration failure is not REQUIRED or ENABLING.

## 6. SPLIT / CLUSTER

### SPLIT when materially independent

Examples:

- operator-visible behavior or acceptance result;
- authoritative owner;
- durable lifecycle or temporal rule;
- safety/failure boundary;
- dependency that can fail independently;
- change cadence or policy authority;
- reusable behavior required by more than one thin-slice path;
- independent empirical question.

Do not split merely because implementation crosses modules, endpoints, tables, or UI components.

### CLUSTER when substantially shared

Prefer clustering where there is one cohesive:

- operator outcome/acceptance specimen;
- semantic model;
- authoritative owner;
- state lifecycle;
- invariant/failure boundary;
- reconciliation question;

and separation would create duplicated representations or artificial handoffs.

A cluster may span persistence, backend, API, browser, observability, and UX.

## 7. Capability-completeness test

The landscape is complete enough when every step in every frozen specimen can truthfully answer:

1. Who or what owns the input fact?
2. How is the subject identified?
3. Which governed context and Program/version apply, if required?
4. What happens when evidence/governance is missing?
5. What behavior transforms the input into the next state?
6. What durable state must survive restart?
7. What identity connects adjacent steps?
8. What can the operator observe?
9. What proves later action/outcome did not rewrite earlier historical truth?
10. Which capability/cluster owns each answer?
11. Is any load-bearing responsibility unowned or multiply authoritative?
12. Can negative/refusal specimens traverse the same path truthfully?

Completeness means **no load-bearing gap in the chosen path**. It does not mean implementing every semantic concept or future lifecycle.

## 8. Integration-contract skeleton

Before dependent cluster designs independently freeze, establish load-bearing semantic obligations for:

- account/capital-scope identity where required;
- Decision Subject identity;
- governed context / Program identity and version binding where required;
- canonical Decision input/output boundary;
- effective/decided/acted/recorded temporal distinctions where required;
- evidence provenance and unknown/insufficient states;
- Recommendation identity;
- Operator Disposition linkage;
- Action/execution linkage only when inside the selected endpoint;
- replay/retrieval expectations;
- refusal and compatibility semantics.

A contract is a semantic obligation, not necessarily a standalone interface or document.

Two clusters may not independently define the same load-bearing authority/identity contract. Shared authoritative primitives must have a single owner before dependent designs freeze.

Do not generalize ordinary utilities into frameworks merely because two implementations look structurally similar.

## 9. Decomposition completion gate

Before the first capability/cluster enters design, the decomposition package must contain:

1. frozen thin-slice outcome contract;
2. authority map;
3. responsibility landscape;
4. end-to-end traceability from specimens to owners;
5. REQUIRED/ENABLING dependency graph;
6. integration-contract skeleton;
7. shared-primitive dispositions;
8. acceptance plan;
9. risk/learning plan including earliest safe observable proof and stop/redirect conditions;
10. completeness attestation showing no unowned or multiply authoritative load-bearing responsibility.

This is the decomposition package, not the individual cluster designs.

## 10. Capability-design completion gate

Before implementing a capability/cluster, require sufficient design to establish:

- exact Product behavior and acceptance specimens;
- authority/state owner;
- semantic inputs/outputs;
- explicit unknown/refusal/degradation behavior;
- durable vs derived vs presentational state;
- temporal/effective/version semantics where applicable;
- upstream/downstream integration contracts;
- invariants and concrete failure modes;
- dependency assumptions;
- technical realization boundaries;
- explicit non-goals;
- test strategy;
- observable learning checkpoint;
- disposition of any genuinely unresolved Product/architecture issue;
- confirmation that remaining choices are ordinary engineering discretion.

A design need not prescribe every class, table, method, DTO, endpoint, or widget.

## 11. Anti-recursive-governance rule

### Reconciliation is required when a decision changes or leaves unresolved

- operator-visible meaning or accepted outcome;
- admissible Alternatives or Recommendation policy;
- authority ownership/precedence;
- durable semantic identity;
- temporal/historical truth;
- trust/safety/failure behavior;
- ratified architecture/ADR boundary;
- thin-slice scope;
- cross-capability invariant;
- promotion of discovery/research into governing Product policy.

### Ordinary design judgment applies when

- choosing bounded representation within settled semantics;
- defining module responsibilities without moving authority;
- selecting transport/storage shapes consistent with accepted invariants;
- specifying required validation, idempotency, or observability mechanics;
- grouping implementation work without changing Product behavior.

### Ordinary implementation discretion applies when

- naming classes/functions/DTOs/tables/migrations/endpoints/components;
- selecting local algorithms whose observable semantics are fixed;
- refactoring internally without moving authority;
- adding tests/instrumentation/error handling/performance work inside the accepted boundary.

A settled boundary reopens only for a concrete correctness, safety, integrity, reversibility, experiment-validity, or authority contradiction—not because another valid design is imaginable.

When reconciliation is actually required, close it using the project's existing reconciliation completion semantics so it produces a durable disposition and next-authorized mode. Do **not** manufacture a reconciliation event for every cluster.

## 12. Walking end-to-end specimen

Implementation order follows earliest safe learning and risk retirement, not technical layers.

For each required cluster:

1. design to the required level;
2. reconcile only material unresolved issues;
3. implement through the walking end-to-end path;
4. independently accept the cluster;
5. rerun cumulative integration specimens;
6. classify checkpoint result as:
   - PROCEED;
   - ADAPT local design/instrumentation within authority;
   - REDIRECT only on a concrete contradiction requiring changed authority/scope/semantics.

Integration is therefore continuous evidence, not a final assembly step.

## 13. Final integrated acceptance

The completed thin slice must be accepted against the original frozen outcome contract, including positive and adverse/refusal specimens, restart/replay expectations where required, and the Principal-visible operator outcome.

Implementation completion is not Product acceptance.

Learning after acceptance may inform future reconciliation. It does not automatically expand the thin slice into deferred Wheelwright scope.

## 14. Known omission and over-promotion risks

The independent reviews converged that code-first decomposition is especially likely to omit:

- authority/provenance;
- Program/Context binding where required;
- temporal applicability;
- explicit insufficiency/unknown states;
- stable Decision identity;
- replay;
- separation of Recommendation, Operator Disposition, Action, execution, and outcome.

They also converged that decomposition is especially likely to over-promote:

- the full semantic ontology;
- generalized Mandate allocation;
- Return Disposition as a premature new entity;
- generic policy/rule engines;
- full lifecycle/outcome identity beyond the selected endpoint;
- Training Mode;
- sandbox execution absent endpoint necessity and authority;
- provider generalization;
- future strategy breadth.

These are risks to test, not automatic exclusions.

## 15. Principal decision boundary

This record adopts the **method**.

It does not decide:

- the actual thin-slice outcome;
- which candidate capabilities exist;
- which capabilities are REQUIRED/ENABLING/DEFER;
- whether sandbox execution belongs in the endpoint;
- unresolved Program/Mandate policy;
- any new semantic entity;
- any implementation design;
- any implementation authorization.

The next decomposition action must therefore begin by freezing the thin-slice outcome contract rather than by enumerating capabilities.
