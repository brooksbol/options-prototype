## Frozen candidate resolutions (before performance inspection)

Daily signals use information available after session close. All orders fill at the next observed **exchange session open**, with adverse slippage and per-share commission. Signals never fill at the signal close. On the final close orders remain pending, never fabricated as fills. Core 100 shares never sold. A and B are binary, exactly 100 shares each. No extra blocks or automatic dividend reinvestment. Benchmark A buys maximum affordable whole shares; Benchmark B buys 300; all at the same first open, initial $100, 000 and same transaction-cost assumptions. Orders cannot overdraw settled cash.

Slow engine: trailing 252-session adjusted **high** (not close), drawdown computed from adjusted close; SMA 200 and 10 strictly-below closes for Level 1; Level 2 requires 25% drawdown and Level 1 already established on an earlier signal session. Level 1 prohibits A; Level 2 prohibits A and B. Slow recovery 1: 5 strictly-above SMA 50 closes. Recovery 1 can occur only once per slow bear episode; it cannot repeatedly restore both blocks without Recovery 2. From Level 2 restore permission for B; from Level 1 restore permission for A. Recovery 2: 5 strictly-above SMA 200; overrides recovery 1 sequencing and clears both slow restrictions. Baseline can restore both blocks at one open; sensitivity limits restoration to one block per session (B first). If only one block was sold, restore only that block.

Hysteresis: slow bear conditions use edge/rearm latches (each condition must become false before it can trigger again) plus a five-session minimum between opposite slow transitions. Recovery counters are reset after slow state transitions. A renewed armed bear event takes priority over recovery. Level 2 has its own drawdown rearm latch. Persistent drawdown alone cannot repeatedly cancel a recovery; this is an explicit candidate resolution, with persistent-bear-priority sensitivity. No same-close Level 1+2 promotion. This latch rule may restore exposure while the underlying remains deeply below its high; test consequences rather than assume it beneficial.

Fast engine: 5-session adjusted close return at/below negative price threshold. Volatility confirmation, when enabled, is `RVX(t)/RVX(t-5)-1 >= threshold`. Both signals must coexist, not merely occur somewhere in a trailing window. Only A is restricted. Missing current/reference RVX prevents a vol-confirmed activation and prevents volatility-based recovery; price-only runs do not depend on RVX. Missing RVX is never interpolated. Repeated activation requires the qualifying condition to first become false. Fast restrictions can activate while slow already prohibits A; the independent veto then matters at restoration.

Fast recovery uses pre-trigger close at t-5 and RVX at t-5 as fixed episode anchors; running low price and high RVX update causally on each close. Recover price by p fraction of `(anchor price - running low)` and reverse v fraction of `(running RVX peak - anchor RVX)`. A two-close confirmation is required. Price-only mode uses price recovery alone. No future bottom/peak. Fast activation takes priority over fast recovery and over a simultaneous slow restoration. Slow and fast vetoes combine: A restored only when BOTH permit; B only depends on slow. Each fast episode logs anchor/low/peak and recovery progress. Extended volatility spikes can delay re-entry.

Portfolio state includes slow level, fast veto, actual A/B holdings, and settled/unsettled cash. Indicator authority and execution status stay separate; capital denial never claims restored shares. Insufficient cash blocks an entire 100-share purchase, logs the denial, and retries next session if permission still holds. Sales settle T+2 until 2024-05-28, T+1 thereafter; core cash never borrowed. Dividend entitlements accrue for pre-open holdings on ex-date, contribute to NAV as receivables, and become spendable seven sessions later (pay dates unavailable; explicit approximation). Cash earns 0% baseline, 2% and 4% constant-rate sensitivity, not an observed historical yield. No taxes; ETF expenses embedded in prices.

## Data and temporal rules

Preferred ThetaData checked first. If terminal/credentials unavailable, use explicitly named Yahoo IWM and FRED/Cboe RVX fallback; do not label it ThetaData. Persist exact responses and SHA 256 manifest. Use exchange XNYS calendar for session validation. Never drop missing IWM sessions into a compressed timeline; fail validation. RVX absence flags remain on the full IWM calendar. Partial-session/current-day observations excluded. Prefix tests verify indicator and action invariance to future-price perturbation.

Executable OHLC is reconstructed historical price: undo Yahoo future split adjustments in OHLC and dividend amounts, then causally adjust signals on split dates. IWM split 2: 1 on 2005-06-09 is preserved in normalized data; main simulation starts 2005-06-10. This is the longest available unchanged 100-share-sleeve interval; pre-split trading requires an explicitly designed resizing policy and is not simulated. Dividend-adjusted OHLC is delivered for reference, reconstructed by scaling raw OHLC by vendor adj-close/close; **never** used as execution prices. Signals use a forward, causal dividend-neutralized index: factor accumulates `split(t)/(1-dividend(t)/(close(t-1)/split(t)))` at each ex-date. Only already-known dividends affect past/current signals; vendor back-adjusted values serve as a ratio-consistency check. This is not a literal tradeable instrument. Dividend cash and NAV are counted exactly once.
