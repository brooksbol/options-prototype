## 2. Evidence-state vocabulary

Retain the source report's evidence labels:

- **VERIFIED** — directly inspected source in the investigation.
- **IDENTIFIED-TRACEABLE** — named source located and traceable, but not directly read at the required primary-source level.
- **INFERENCE** — analyst synthesis or extrapolation rather than a source-established fact.
- **ABSENCE-OF-EVIDENCE** — adequate evidence searched for but not found.

Evidence status must travel with claims. Repetition does not upgrade evidence. A downstream task may upgrade a claim only by actually satisfying the stronger evidence standard.

## 3. Research-program invariants

1. Practitioner education is an object of study, not independent empirical confirmation.
2. Shared ancestry must not be counted as independent replication.
3. Population boundaries travel with evidence: index, single-name, asset class, jurisdiction, participant type, tenor, regime, and period must not be silently generalized.
4. Gross return is not net executable return.
5. Midpoint marks are not executable fills.
6. Trade-level expectancy is not sequential portfolio expectancy.
7. Win rate is not expectancy.
8. Backtest association is not causal mechanism.
9. Historical market structure is not automatically current market structure.
10. Negative or null results redirect the research program; they do not authorize parameter mining.
11. Contradictions and absence of evidence are findings, not gaps to fill with inference.
12. Primary evidence is preferred for load-bearing premises.
13. Research results do not become Product policy merely by being preserved or verified.
14. Domain research assignments remain free of Wheelwright-specific architecture, ontology, Product policy, implementation needs, or desired answers unless a later, explicit reconciliation task requires them.

## 4. Independent evidence lineages

The source report's fourteen vectors are treated operationally as evidence surfaces whose independence must be assessed rather than as fourteen votes.

The main independent lineages are:

- academic econometrics;
- exchange/vendor mechanics;
- regulatory measurement;
- institutional/professional practice;
- historical record.

Practitioner-education lineage is primarily the object of study. Data infrastructure enables research but is not itself evidence.

## 5. Long-horizon pipeline

The source report proposes:

- **Wave 0 — Evidence feasibility:** dataset × question map, canon evidence grades, lineage graph.
- **Wave 1 — Anchors and falsifiers:** net-execution baseline, VRP boundary matrix, strategy-index autopsy, benchmark tribunal.
- **Wave 2 — Aggregation, survival, capital:** correlated tails, capital occupation, assignment/exercise drag.
- **Wave 3 — Mechanisms:** dealer positioning and option returns, 21-DTE causal adjudication, crowding/erosion.
- **Wave 4 — Regime and structure:** 0DTE transfer, retail-loss attribution, regulatory/enforcement winner-side evidence.
- **Wave 5 — Portfolio role and alternatives:** matched-risk alternatives, inventory-risk theory, evidence-bounded design implications.

These waves are conditional. A later wave is not authorized merely because it appears in the plan. Earlier results may redirect, narrow, invalidate, or reorder later work.

## 8. Q1 branch commitment

If, after primary verification and preregistration, Q1 eventually yields a credible result:

### Branch A — robustly positive net retail expectancy

Next research emphasis: **portfolio aggregation, survival, capital occupation, and correlated-tail behavior.**

A positive trade-level result does not establish a positive portfolio-level result.

### Branch B — null or negative net retail expectancy

Next research emphasis: **premium incidence / who captures the documented premium and through which mechanical channels**, including execution costs, intermediary economics, participant classes, night/weekend incidence, and regulatory/enforcement evidence.

A null/negative result does not authorize searching parameter space until a profitable variant appears.

### Branch C — result not credible or not identifiable

Do not force either branch. Record the identification/evidence failure and return to the missing prerequisite.

## 9. Preregistration gate

After primary-source upgrade, but before observing Q1 results, freeze a protocol covering at minimum:

- exact strategy definitions;
- instruments and populations;
- date range and regime partitions;
- data sources;
- entry and exit mechanics;
- assignment/exercise handling;
- dividends and corporate actions where applicable;
- fill model;
- commission and fee model;
- spread/slippage model;
- capital/collateral assumptions;
- overlapping-position treatment;
- benchmark definitions;
- metrics and confidence intervals;
- sensitivity analyses;
- missing-data treatment;
- success/failure criteria;
- branch conditions.

Independent adversarial review should occur before execution. Material post-result specification changes require explicit disclosure and must not be represented as preregistered.

## 10. Completion discipline

A research stage completes only when its stated gate is satisfied or when inability to satisfy the gate is itself durably recorded as a finding.

Research should maximize cumulative information value, not task volume.

Parallel work is appropriate when tracks are genuinely independent. Dependencies must be respected when an upstream result determines a downstream population, model, benchmark, or hypothesis.

## 11. Low-information work currently deprioritized

Unless new evidence changes the information-value calculation, do not spend the next research cycle on:

- another isolated SPY-strangle DTE tweak;
- optimizing 45/50/21 before net execution is established;
- ML option-surface prediction without executable-cost modeling;
- additional retail-educator interviews merely adding repetitions of existing lineage;
- narrative retellings of historical episodes without a common falsification template;
- entry-filter optimization detached from exit, friction, aggregation, and capital constraints.

## 12. Research-state transition rule

Every consequential transition should preserve:

1. prior research state;
2. evidence that satisfied or failed the current gate;
3. claims upgraded, downgraded, falsified, or left unresolved;
4. resulting branch decision;
5. exact next research question;
6. why that question now has the highest information value.

Do not rewrite prior states with hindsight.
