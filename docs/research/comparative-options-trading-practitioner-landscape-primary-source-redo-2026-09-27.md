## 2. Consensus Inventory (per-phrase, with the false-consensus matrix)

The study tested "surface agreement" claims term by term. The headline result: several high-recognition phrases are in use across corpora but **do not mean the same thing**, and several phrases appear in only a subset of corpora.

### 2.1 Phrase-by-phrase audit

**"Sell premium."** In use at: tastylive (core operating doctrine, undefined-risk strangles; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION); Option Alpha (2017 identity page: "selling options is the name of the game"; later: the *only* EV-positive program is the separate 0DTE SPX program; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN / TRANSCRIPT); ProjectOption (SPY put-selling research program, 41,600-trade study; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN); InTheMoney (income wheel on single names; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION); SMB (a *subset* of the workshop: Practice A/B sells premium selectively; the flagship Rhino is also short premium but signal-conditioned; the one-day product sells premium; the casino exemplar is a debit spread; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT). **Not in use at:** SpotGamma (Kochuba's disclosed positions are long-convexity structures; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT); Bassman (net buyer of long-dated rate convexity, though a conditional seller at shorter tenors; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN); Lakha (volatility is bought or sold by carry/richness; "sell premium" is not a default; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN). **Verdict: majority phrase, not universal. FALSE CONSENSUS** — the same words cover systematic inventory (tasty), one of several programs (OA), income-tooling on single names (InTheMoney), and selective/signal-conditioned selling (SMB).

**"Trade small."** tastylive: stated (allocation guidance, e.g., ~5%-of-account buying-power guidance in official docs); PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN for the current era, SECONDARY — TRACEABLE for founder-era. InTheMoney: repeated as advice to small accounts but practiced by rolling single-name wheels; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. SMB (Freudberg): **contradicts** — "it was way too small"; he argues small accounts should chase 20–30% winners through a proven strategy rather than accept 2–3% small-portfolio income; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. Lakha: "Don't be a hero — this is a game of survival," small position sizing, Kelly for 0DTE; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN. Bassman: "sizing is more important than entry level" — the durable master rule; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. **Verdict: widespread but contradicted by Freudberg at the account-size level. FALSE CONSENSUS** — the phrase is generic advice; practitioners diverge on what "small" means and whether it works.

**"High probability."** tastylive: the POP-over-the-trade-life doctrine (e.g., "we want to be winning at least 70% of the time"); PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. ProjectOption: presents the 16Δ/50%-target studies as the empirical home of high-probability; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN. OA's automation era: **explicitly rejects win-rate as the objective** — the SPX 0DTE program runs <50% win rates deliberately; win rate is a tunable parameter inside an EV/NVRP objective; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. SMB one-day product: ~67% win rate; the Rhino product is adjusted, not high-POP; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. SpotGamma/Bassman/Lakha: do not traffic in POP; Kochuba talks expected value and regime payoffs; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. **Verdict: a tastytrade-cluster phrase; rejected by OA's current program. FALSE CONSENSUS.**

**"Manage winners / take profit early (50%)."** tastylive: 50% of max profit, combined with 21-DTE exit; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION (item 2; also official docs). ProjectOption: 50%-target studies exist, but its *own internal falsifier* (41,600-trade study) found holding to expiration had the highest average P/L per trade ($53 vs $35 at 90%), with early management winning only on a non-sequential "45-day-adjusted" capital-recycling extrapolation that Butler called "not perfect"; the iron-condor study found 25% targets went negative after commissions and 50–75% best for 16Δ; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN. OA early era: 50%-of-profit *per bot cycle*, not per trade; SPX 0DTE-era logic differs; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN. InTheMoney: 50% take-profit on the *original* premium (his wording also admits 25%); PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. SMB/OA-0DTE/SpotGamma/Bassman/Lakha: no such doctrine; exit rules are mostly unobserved or regime-driven. **Verdict: tasty-cluster mechanical rule; contradicted inside ProjectOption's own corpus by per-trade P/L evidence. FALSE CONSENSUS.**

**"Be the casino."** **The slogan that means the most different things.** tastylive: the house-edge analogy attached to short-premium market-making logic; SECONDARY — TRACEABLE in the sampled corpus (verbatim "be the casino" not found in the three sampled primary transcripts; attribution labeled accordingly). Option Alpha: **PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN (Kirk, 2017 "options trading blog" page)** — "every single transaction ... has a positive expected value ... this is exactly the same business model as the casino"; his 2014–2021 program is explicitly the attempt to make the casino metaphor operational through backtests, sizing, and repetition. SMB: **PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT** — Freudberg's "be the casino" means *any positive-expectancy backtested system, on either side*: his explicit exemplar is a **directional debit spread** (long premium) that produced casino-like statistics; the metaphor attaches to tested edge + repetition + risk management, not to selling options. Bassman: uses "casino" as a **strategy description** (a systematic VIX-income strategy he recommends to investors), not an identity; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. Lakha: **explicitly rejects** the casino model (2022 note: "I don't buy the casino premium capture model"); PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN. **Verdict: three different operational definitions across tasty/OA/SMB; one rejection (Lakha); one demotion to strategy-name (Bassman). The phrase is evidence of NOTHING shared. This is the study's clearest false-consensus specimen.** See H3.

**"Cost basis."** InTheMoney: original cost-basis doctrine (adjusted basis including premium), then a **2023 self-correction** demoting adjusted basis to "psychological comfort"; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION (self-falsifier). ProjectOption: cost-basis framing for premium collected ("lower your cost basis") on the wheel page; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN. OA: **PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT** — capacity-first "bucket" logic ("the only thing that matters is the balance in the account"), effectively anti-cost-basis. tastylive: not present as doctrine in observed items. **Verdict: shared vocabulary, opposed accounting. FALSE CONSENSUS.**

**"Income."** InTheMoney: "a money machine that prints money"; income-first, assignment-avoidant; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. ProjectOption: **explicitly rejects** "trading for income" as a framing (income framing ≠ income generation); PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN. tastylive: "income" appears but the corpus frames expectancy and probability of trade life, not paycheck cadence. OA automation: return-on-capital EV, not income. Bassman: pension-fund-style yield replacement is a *client-objective*, not his objective (his is convexity/insurance). **Verdict: the word travels; the objective differs. FALSE CONSENSUS.**

**"Roll for a credit / roll to avoid assignment."** InTheMoney: roll ITM puts for additional credit, avoid assignment; PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. tastylive: rolling doctrine present in current materials (older founder-era form); SECONDARY — TRACEABLE for specifics. ProjectOption: roll-avoidance mentioned; PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN. OA: rolling present in early conditional-exit era; later eras differ. **Verdict: genuine shared *mechanical* practice among retail premium sellers — but motives differ (avoid-assignment vs. objective-continuation), and ProjectOption's research found roll-for-credit in IC studies was not obviously optimal. PARTIAL CONSENSUS (mechanics, not doctrine).**

**"Mechanical / systematic."** tastylive: the canon is mechanical (45/50/21); PRIMARY. OA: mechanical only within the automation era's bot frameworks; the early era was rules-with-discretion; the SPX 0DTE program is a rule-set; PRIMARY. ProjectOption: research-driven but allows analyst discretion ("subjective" calls); PRIMARY. InTheMoney: discretionary within loose rules (he rolls, overrides); PRIMARY. SMB: mixed — some products mechanical, Freudberg's own practice discretionary; PRIMARY. SpotGamma/Lakha: discretionary reads of maps/surfaces; PRIMARY. Bassman: structural, not mechanical. **Verdict: "systematic" describes an aspiration and a marketing posture more than a shared practice. FALSE CONSENSUS as a doctrine; PARTIAL as a product feature.**

## 4. Hypothesis Audit (H1–H9)

Each hypothesis was scored against the primary corpus only. Verdicts: **CONFIRMED** / **CONFIRMED — bounded** / **MIXED** / **NOT SUPPORTED** / **FALSIFIED** / **UNRESOLVED**.

### H1 — "45/50/21 is predominantly tastylive/Market Measures lineage; never published as one specification; later imports are documented."

**CONFIRMED — with two refinements.** The composite canon is tastylive's: primary item 2 tests 50%-profit + 21-day-exit management explicitly and it wins on expectancy; item C5 shows the components arrive as givens with only the profit target visibly swept; current official docs teach the triple together. **Refinement 1:** the components were studied separately — "Enter at 45 DTE, Exit at 21 DTE" aired as a *doctrine* segment on Jul 27, 2020 (per the verified spintwig/brief compilation), and the spintwig independent replication (54 backtests, >170,500 trades, 2007–2019) runs 30Δ/50%-or-21-DTE on SPY/QQQ/IWM — replication of the composite, not independent derivation of the constants. **Refinement 2:** the 30-delta guideline is a house guideline, not a study constant — the observed studies use 16Δ and delta grids; OA's one import (Jack's "Tasty Condor" bot, PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION) explicitly credits Tasty and applies only to that item; OA's *own* early canon was 30–40 DTE with conditional exits. ProjectOption teaches 45-DTE entries and ~50% targets (documented tasty lineage) but **no 21-DTE rule was found in its corpus** — the canon did not transfer whole even inside the lineage. InTheMoney's 0.3Δ/30–45 DTE/50%-take-profit wheel is SIMILARITY ONLY (no citation). SMB/SpotGamma/Bassman/Lakha: no trace of the canon. The "never published as one specification" phrasing needs the 2020 caveat, but the underlying studies were separate and no single study document publishes the full spec.

### H2 — "The premium-selling 'consensus' is partly one idea with many megaphones."

**MIXED — the hypothesis is too coarse; the pair-level truth is documented.** *Shared-megaphone* pairs: tastytrade → ProjectOption (DOCUMENTED INFLUENCE; employment + citation + marketing agreement); tastytrade → OA's Tasty Condor bot (DOCUMENTED, item-specific). *Independent-derivation* pairs: OA's early program (own backtests, different DTE/exits, its own earnings falsifier); InTheMoney's wheel (Robinhood-era, no citation, SIMILARITY ONLY); Freudberg's SMB (insurance-career origin, expectancy-pluralist; "casino" means any tested system). *Falsifier inside the cluster*: ProjectOption's own 41,600-trade study contradicts manage-early as an unconditional optimum. So the "consensus" is a real mechanical overlap (sell premium on liquid underlyings, IV-gated, defined-risk) arrived at by at least three independent routes plus one documented lineage — not one idea with many megaphones, and not fully independent convergence either. The hypothesis as stated is **NOT SUPPORTED in its strong form**; its weak form (some of the consensus is shared lineage) is **CONFIRMED**. Verdict: **MIXED**, pair-dependent, per the lineage graph in §3.2.

### H3 — "'Be the casino' independently converges from pits/insurance/institutional-desks traditions."

**NOT SUPPORTED as a convergence claim; the slogan is false-consensus language.** What the primary corpus actually shows: (a) OA's Kirk operationalized the casino metaphor in 2017 in practitioner-written text (PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN) and built his 2014–2021 program around making it true via backtests — this is the *strongest* documented casino doctrine in the corpus; (b) Freudberg's "be the casino" (PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT) means any positive-expectancy tested system **including a directional debit spread** — operationally incompatible with (a); he invokes his insurance CEO past as analogy, not lineage, and no institutional-desk origin is evidenced; (c) tastylive's pit/market-making ancestry is real background but the verbatim "be the casino" line was not found in the three sampled primary transcripts (SECONDARY — TRACEABLE; gap labeled); (d) Lakha **explicitly rejects** the casino model; (e) Bassman demotes "casino" to a strategy name. The convergence is on the *word*, not the behavior. The pits→casino and desks→casino arrows are UNRESOLVED at best. If the hypothesis means "the slogan appears in several corpora," it is CONFIRMED; if it means "they independently converged on the same operating idea," it is FALSIFIED by Freudberg's debit-spread exemplar and Lakha's rejection. Overall: **MIXED, leaning NOT SUPPORTED** for the convergence interpretation that carries the explanatory weight.

### H4 — "InTheMoney teaches an acquisition-first Wheel (shares first, premium last)."

**FALSIFIED — for every era with primary evidence (2020 → early 2023).** Three primary utterances, all TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION: (1) "We do not want to get assigned, we are looking to generate as much income as possible" (and in the same segment: "I'm sure many of you would just want the 4.5%, but we have a way to potentially get a bit more juice"); (2) the self-correcting monologue: "it would have been much better just to keep that 50 bucks and not have to be assigned ... it's a two-sided coin"; (3) "the goal ... is to ... collect that premium and avoid assignment." His doctrine is **income-first, assignment-avoidant**, with stock ownership as a *consent/risk constraint*, not an objective. The acquisition-first claim traced to exactly one source: an August 2026 video *description* (not speech) for a video absent from the channel's public listing — UNRESOLVED, and insufficient to sustain the hypothesis against ten transcripts. 2024–2026 wheel doctrine remains UNRESOLVED (the recent primary items are LEAPS-buying, not wheel-running). **Verdict: FALSIFIED** (2020–2023, primary); the 2026 description is not primary evidence and does not revive it.

### H5 — "ProjectOption's 41,600-trade study is an internal falsifier of manage-early."

**CONFIRMED — bounded, and narrower than the register implied.** The study (PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN, archived): SPY, 16Δ puts, standard expiration nearest 45 DTE, 2007–May 2017, n = 41,600. Finding: holding to expiration had the **highest average P/L per trade** ($53 at $500 margin; 10th percentile –$1,900 — i.e., right tail of losses). Early exits (5%/25%/50%/75%) won only after a **non-sequential "45-day-adjusted" capital-recycling extrapolation** that Butler explicitly called "not perfect" — it is arithmetic, not a sequential redeployment backtest. Two further bounds: (a) commissions were *mentioned* as drag but **not modeled** in this study; (b) the companion iron-condor study *did* model $1/contract ($8 round trip) and found **25% profit targets went negative after commissions**, with 50–75% best for 16Δ. So H5 confirms: inside the premium-selling cluster, the corpus's own flagship study falsifies manage-early as an *unconditional* optimum — but the falsification is per-trade gross P/L, and the corpus's own resolution is objective-dependence (per-trade P/L vs. capital recycling vs. commission-adjusted expectancy), not a victory for either side. **Verdict: CONFIRMED (bounded).**

### H6 — "SpotGamma/Kochuba is a genuine market-structure outlier."

**CONFIRMED.** ~295 minutes of Kochuba speaking plus four practitioner-written posts: dealer-gamma regimes, vanna/charm, OPEX, skew-percentile timing, 0DTE flow, forced hedging. His disclosed positions are predominantly **long-convexity defined-risk structures** (VIX call spreads, put flies, calendars/diagonals) — the inventory of a premium *buyer*. The slogan audit inverts on every retail-premium slogan; his "casino" is the dealer, and the retail trader's edge is reading the dealer's forced flows. tastylive distributes the work but frames it explicitly as "a fresh perspective" — distribution, not doctrinal absorption. Bounds: lifecycle/management rules largely unobserved; no performance evidence; one promising tastylive segment inaccessible. The outlier designation does not depend on performance claims and survives the bounds. **Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

### H7 — "Bassman is a genuine long-optionality falsifier of premium selling."

**NOT SUPPORTED in the broad form; CONFIRMED in a narrowed conditional form.** The broad "anti-premium-selling long-vol outlier" claim is false on primary evidence: Bassman calls premium selling "a fine strategy" when sized properly (PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION); he personally **sold 1–3-year rate options against his 10-year long**; he recommends a systematic short-VIX-futures carry strategy with crash protection (2024 model portfolio: 30% short-VIX carry). What survives is narrow and precise: a sustained, conditional doctrine of **selective very-long-dated rate-option convexity as portfolio insurance** — 5–10-year payer swaptions, 4.25% strike on correlation-flip logic, slow theta, collateral carry, "sizing is more important than entry level" as master rule. He rejects *equity* tail hedges as structurally overpriced and rates hedges as fairly priced — a rates/equity boundary the broad claim erased. **Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED (broad); CONFIRMED (narrowed: conditional long-dated rate convexity + conditional premium selling).**

### H8 — "Lakha/Options Insight is a volatility-surface relative-value practitioner."

**CONFIRMED.** 23 items, 2021–2026, five years of stability: ETH-vs-BTC straddle RV, rates term-structure calendars, skew-aware strike selection, carry as the gamma buy/sell switch ("When vol's rich, don't buy too much VEGA. ## 8. Empirical-Claim Audit I — index vs. single-name VRP, correlation risk, skew/jumps, earnings, option returns, liquidity

This section audits the load-bearing empirical claims of the retail premium-selling program against outside evidence. Each paper is reported with population, instruments, period, and assumptions; applicability statements are labeled.

### 8.1 The index premium (support)

- **Coval & Shumway (2001), RFS**: zero-cost ATM straddle positions on the S&P 500 (Jan 1990–Oct 1995) produce average losses of ~3% per week — the canonical index-volatility-selling result. SECONDARY — TRACEABLE (via a peer-reviewed review chapter citing the paper; original not directly inspected).
- **Bakshi & Kapadia (2003), RFS**: S&P 500 options (Jan 1988–Dec 1995), 14–60-day maturities, ±10% moneyness; delta-hedged gains significantly negative; "the underperformance is greater at times of higher volatility." SECONDARY — TRACEABLE.
- **IV-over-RV on SPY/QQQ/IWM**: a retail replication (GitHub, ba2tradeplatform) reports implied-minus-realized ≈ 1.43 vol points on index ETFs vs. ≈ 1.87 on 91 single names — i.e., IV-over-RV is present in both, slightly larger in single names in vol points. SECONDARY — WEAK (retail replication, unverified methodology). Included as a complication: IV-minus-RV in vol points is not the variance risk premium, and the *significance* of the premium (Carr & Wu §8.2) is a different question.
- **Drift confound**: an independent retail replication (dobrowolski, Medium, Apr 2026) finds SPY put spreads theoretically negative-EV in every bucket under flat-vol pricing but realized positive average P/L everywhere — attributing the realized edge to the SPY equity drift, i.e., realized premium-selling P/L on indexes includes the equity risk premium, not pure VRP. SECONDARY — WEAK (retail, unverified) — but the *point* is a standard econometric caution (delta exposure ≠ variance exposure) and is consistent with Bakshi–Kapadia's delta-hedged framing. INFERENCE: treat unhedged backtests as joint tests of VRP + drift.

### 8.2 The single-name boundary (the load-bearing audit)

- **Driessen, Maenhout & Vilkov (2009), Journal of Finance, "The Price of Correlation Risk: Evidence from Equity Options"**: 17-year sample; large negative *index* variance risk premium; **no evidence of a negative risk premium on individual variance risk**. A companion result (cited review): the implied-minus-realized correlation difference is significantly positive for S&P 500 and DJ30 — a significantly negative price of *correlation* risk. Interpretation: the index VRP is largely a correlation-risk price; selling "diversified" single-name premium does not diversify away the priced risk. SECONDARY — TRACEABLE (abstract + review citations; original full text not directly inspected).
- **Carr & Wu (2009), RFS, "Variance Risk Premiums"**: SPX 30-day variance swaps — mean return −$2.74 per $100 notional, Sharpe 0.98. Of **35 individual stocks, only 7 generate variance risk premia significantly negative at the 95% confidence level**; individual results are much smaller with wider variation. SECONDARY — TRACEABLE (via a traceable book-chapter quotation of the paper's results; original not directly inspected).
- **Goyal & Saretto (2009), JFE, "Cross-section of option returns and volatility"**: entire U.S. single-stock option market, OptionMetrics, Jan 1996–Dec 2006, 4,344 stocks, 75,627 monthly call/put pairs. A zero-cost long-short decile portfolio of one-month ATM straddles sorted on realized-minus-implied volatility yields **+22.7% monthly average, Sharpe 0.710**; the IV-rich short leg (decile 1) averages −12.8%/month long — i.e., *selection on rich IV within single names* works. **Costs**: at effective quoted spreads the long-short return falls to **3.9%/month**. SECONDARY — TRACEABLE (abstract + EconPapers record + a traceable compilation quoting its tables).
- **Corroborating cross-section**: Cao & Han (2013): delta-hedged option returns negatively related to total and idiosyncratic volatility. SECONDARY — WEAK (via review citation only).

**Audit verdict on the boundary:** The prior register's "triple-confirmed index-vs-single-name VRP boundary" is **confirmed as a boundary claim and must not be read as a zero claim**. What the literature supports: (a) the *unconditional* index variance premium is large, robust, and largely correlation risk (Driessen et al.; Carr & Wu); (b) *unconditional* single-name variance premia are mostly insignificant (Carr & Wu: 7/35); (c) *conditional, cross-sectional* single-name selection on rich-vs-cheap IV has strong support (Goyal & Saretto) — but spread costs destroy most of the paper return. **Retail doctrines map onto this unevenly**: tastylive's IVR-gating maps to the cross-sectional literature better than its unconditional canon does; InTheMoney teaches no IV-gating and no single-name evidence of its own (§8.6); OA's NVRP is the closest retail analogue to the academic selection logic.

### 8.3 Correlation risk, skew, jumps

- **Correlation risk**: Driessen et al. (2009) — correlation risk is priced and drives the index variance premium. **Practitioner consequence**: selling many single names does not diversify the priced risk; ProjectOption's Q&A observation ("apparently independent positions become correlated during volatility") is the retail-phenomenology version of the same fact. INFERENCE, grounded both sides.
- **Skew**: the literature treats risk-neutral skewness as priced (review citations: Dennis & Mayhew 2002; Bali & Murray 2013). Retail practice: Kochuba times entries on 3-month 25-delta put-skew percentiles (PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN); Lakha's strike selection is skew-aware (PRIMARY — PRACTITIONER-WRITTEN); the retail premium schools mostly ignore skew as a priced quantity. **A priced input that the mechanical premium programs do not model.**
- **Jumps**: Broadie–Chernov–Johannes (2009) find index put-selling returns consistent with jump-risk models (no anomaly). SECONDARY — WEAK (review citation). Lakha's "the premium is the stop" and Kochuba's event-convexity buying are the practitioner acknowledgments of jump risk; the retail premium schools' loss-multiple arms (tastylive 2015 study) and 21-DTE exits are the mechanical acknowledgments.

### 8.4 Earnings

- **OA's internal falsifier**: a reported 648,861-trade study led Kirk to **stop trading earnings** — one of the corpus's two large-sample practitioner falsifiers (the other: ProjectOption's 41,600). PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION (the study itself was not independently audited).
- **InTheMoney's opposite practice**: explicitly sells premium *into* earnings for IV crush, on single names — PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT/CAPTION. This is a genuine cross-corpus contradiction on identical mechanics (short premium into a known event), resolved only by noting the different risk stances (InTheMoney accepts gap risk for crush; OA's data rejected it).
- **Academic side**: the earnings-announcement volatility literature documents announcement premia and post-announcement crush; no single paper was directly inspected for this audit — the practitioner contradiction is reported as primary fact, the academic adjudication is UNRESOLVED.

### 8.5 Option returns and liquidity

- **Option returns**: the academic record studies *delta-hedged* or *variance-swap* returns (Bakshi–Kapadia; Carr–Wu; Goyal–Saretto). Retail backtests report *unhedged* option P/L (tastylive, spintwig, ProjectOption, SteadyOptions replication). These are different quantities; the drift confound (§8.1) means retail P/L ≠ VRP harvesting. **Do not cite retail backtests as evidence for the variance premium; do not cite VRP papers as evidence for retail backtest P/L.** INFERENCE — methodological.
- **Liquidity**: Goyal–Saretto's 22.7%/mo → 3.9%/mo at quoted spreads is the peer-reviewed liquidity bound. Retail: OA's 5¢-in/5¢-out slippage stress test "cratered" a backtest (PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT); the Medium replication claims mid-fill backtests overstate CAGR 30–60% (SECONDARY — WEAK); SMB's Weekly Options Income Machine marketed precise returns with liquidity/slippage/commissions *excluded* (PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT); ProjectOption's IC study modeled commissions but no slippage; spintwig's commission assumptions are undisclosed and its detail paywalled (§9). **The literature and the practitioners agree on the direction (costs are the largest threat to the paper edge) and disagree on nothing — but the retail schools model costs inconsistently or not at all.**

### 8.6 Applicability bridge — what the academic evidence actually covers

| Retail claim | Academic coverage | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 45-DTE/50%/21-DTE on SPY | No paper tests this composite. Index VRP papers test variance swaps / delta-hedged options, not the managed construct. | **Uncovered** — the composite is practitioner-empirical only |
| "Selling index premium has positive expectancy" | Supported for delta-hedged/variance exposure (Bakshi–Kapadia; Coval–Shumway; Carr–Wu SPX); unhedged retail backtests are joint tests with drift | **Supported with the drift caveat** |
| "Selling single-name premium has positive expectancy" (unconditional) | Mostly insignificant (Carr–Wu 7/35); no negative individual variance risk premium (Driessen et al.) | **Not supported unconditionally** |
| "Sell the IV-rich single names" (IV-gated) | Strong cross-sectional support, pre-cost (Goyal–Saretto); costs remove most of it | **Supported conditionally, cost-bounded** |
| "Diversify across many names" | Correlation risk is the priced component (Driessen et al.); tails correlate (ProjectOption Q&A) | **The diversification claim is backwards for the priced risk** |

## 9. Empirical-Claim Audit II — transaction costs (incl. the spintwig 20.77% claim)

### 9.1 The spintwig claim — traced and bounded

**Traced identity**: spintwig = *"SPY Short Put 45 DTE Cash-Secured Options Backtest"* (published ~Jun 10, 2019; republished Mar 2024): **54 backtests, >170,500 trades, 2007-01-03 → 2019-07-31**, daily entries, 30-delta ±3.5, 45 DTE ±17, management at 50% max profit or 21 DTE (whichever first); SPY plus companion 45-DTE put backtests on QQQ, IWM, AAPL, TSLA, AMZN, "and others." Source: the live site blocks automated access (403); the figure was recovered via a Wayback-archived page quoted verbatim in a traceable research brief (csnyder256/shadow-options-trading-lab, research date 2026-07-19): **"20.77% – the blended average percent of profits spent on commission across all short put strategies."**

**What is verified**: the study's existence, identity, sample (54 variants, >170,500 trades, 2007–2019), the exact management rule, and the verbatim 20.77% sentence. **Status: SECONDARY — TRACEABLE** (not independently verified primary — the underlying commission schedule, per-variant tables, and the gross-vs-net denominator are paywalled; the commission assumptions are undisclosed).

**What is NOT verified**: the commission $/contract assumption (hence whether 20.77% is conservative or aggressive), whether the denominator is gross or net profit, the spread/slippage treatment, and the per-variant dispersion around the 20.77% blend. **The claim must not be quoted as "one-fifth of profits go to commissions" without the bounds**: it is a blended average across 54 variants of one backtester's undisclosed commission model.

**Attribution correction**: the prior register loosely associated large-trade-count cost figures with the practitioner corpora; the 170,500-trade figure belongs to **spintwig, an independent retail backtester — not to ProjectOption** (whose flagship is 41,600 trades) and not to tastylive.

### 9.2 Cost treatment per corpus (primary-observed)

- **tastylive**: the three observed studies report **gross P/L**; item 2 acknowledges a redeployment benefit not captured. No commission/slippage accounting found in the inspected transcripts. (22 further videos pending; the bound may move.)
- **Option Alpha**: the corpus's most cost-honest voice. 2015: "worry less about commissions and more about slippage" — a $0.20 spread example estimated at **$3,840/year**; 2024: **5¢-in/5¢-out slippage "craters"** a profitable backtest ("a zero strategy"); SmartPricing slippage-tolerance in the bot framework. Counter-note: one bot performance page **explicitly excluded commissions** (stated, not hidden).
- **ProjectOption**: short-put study *mentions* commissions as drag, **does not model them**; iron-condor study models **$1/contract ($8 round trip)** — 25% profit targets went **negative** after commissions, 50–75% best for 16Δ. No slippage model anywhere in the inspected corpus.
- **SMB**: the Weekly Options Income Machine's marketed return figures **excluded liquidity, slippage, and commissions** (PRIMARY — TRANSCRIPT) — the corpus's weakest cost treatment.
- **InTheMoney**: limit-order/mid-price guidance; no cost quantification observed.
- **SpotGamma / Bassman / Lakha**: execution-cost treatment thin or absent in the public corpus (institutional-context practitioners; costs are not the product).

### 9.3 Synthesis

The direction is unanimous across literature and practitioners: **friction is the largest threat to paper premium edges**, and its size is on the order of the edge itself (spintwig 20.77% blended commissions; Goyal–Saretto ~80%+ of cross-sectional return at quoted spreads; OA's slippage crater). The modeling is inconsistent: only ProjectOption's IC study and OA's stress tests put numbers inside the decision loop. **No corpus was found that models commissions AND slippage AND the bid/ask realistically in its flagship backtests.** The honest statement of the retail premium edge is: *positive expectancy on liquid index underlyings, gross of friction, with friction consuming a large and assumption-sensitive share.*
