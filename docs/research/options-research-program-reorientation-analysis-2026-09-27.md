## Status and authority

This is a separate Wheelwright analysis of the preserved Muse memo. It does not modify the source artifact.

The memo is **WW-directed research-program synthesis**, not WW-sanitized independent options-domain research. The assignment exposed Wheelwright purpose, internal framing, process identifiers, file paths, and Principal priorities to Muse. Therefore the memo cannot support the claim that an independent domain researcher spontaneously identified these priorities.

That contamination does **not** invalidate the external evidence gathered by the workers. It changes the epistemic status of the synthesis and prioritization:

- external evidence may remain useful according to its own provenance and inspection state;
- the memo's priority ordering is intentionally conditioned on Wheelwright/Principal objectives;
- future independent domain-research assignments should restore the sanitation boundary: options domain → independent research → durable evidence → later WW reconciliation.

## 1. Reorientation judgment

The reorientation succeeds at the objective-function level.

The prior research program was rigorous but increasingly optimized for the next scientifically unresolved question rather than the next operator-consequential question. The chain from VRP → executable VRP → execution friction → missing SPX friction cell → temporal incidence → mechanism adjudication was intellectually coherent but did not require the next answer to change an operator behavior, capital-survival boundary, or capability decision.

The memo's strongest correction is:

> research priority should be determined by consequence to the operator, while evidence strength remains a separate dimension.

This preserves the program's evidence discipline while redirecting information value toward capital preservation, loss of portfolio control, sustainable repeatability, measurement integrity, revision governance, portfolio aggregation, and brokerage capability.

## 2. Forced liquidation: important mechanism, overstrong universalization

The memo's statement that “the ruin object is not trade-level max loss but forced liquidation at the loss extreme under an expanded margin requirement” is too broad.

The evidence supports a narrower and highly important conclusion:

> **Forced liquidation caused by collateral or margin expansion is a distinct path-to-ruin mechanism that trade-level maximum-loss analysis can miss, and it may dominate leveraged short-premium portfolios.**

At least three failure objects must remain distinct:

1. **Economic ruin** — losses impair NAV enough that the operation is no longer viable.
2. **Liquidity/collateral ruin** — the position may have bounded or recoverable terminal economics, but the account cannot finance the path.
3. **Broker-enforced ruin** — broker requirements or liquidation powers remove the operator's disposition authority at an adverse time.

These mechanisms differ materially across naked short options, portfolio-margin books, Reg-T books, defined-risk spreads, cash-secured puts, covered calls, and combinations.

The memo itself implicitly acknowledges the overreach: Q-R1 labels forced liquidation as the binding ruin mechanism an **INFERENCE** and proposes testing whether voluntary exits dominate. Therefore “the ruin object” should not be promoted as established universal domain truth.

## 3. Path survivability is more important than expiration payoff

The underlying discovery is high-value.

An options position can have a known terminal payoff and still be operationally fatal if collateral requirements along the path exceed the account's ability to maintain it. The relevant operator question is not merely:

> Can the account afford the terminal loss?

It is closer to:

> **Can the account continue to own this economic position through a plausible stress path without losing control of disposition?**

This is directly relevant to sustainable options operation and should survive the reorientation.

## 4. Brokerage capability has two sides

The memo materially improves the brokerage question.

Broker evaluation should not be limited to capabilities exposed **to** the operator (spreads, collars, multi-leg orders, APIs, lifecycle tooling, assignment handling, risk visibility). It must also consider powers retained **by** the broker that can affect the operator under stress (margin methodology, house overlays, concentration requirements, liquidation discretion, notification behavior, expiration/assignment handling, operational resilience).

The research therefore supports a capability-oriented question:

> Can the chosen operating process be executed faithfully, and under what broker-controlled conditions can the operator lose control of the position?

Precise fill optimization remains lower priority unless evidence shows it materially changes survival, repeatability, utilization, or economic results at the relevant scale.

## 5. Reserve empirics should be reframed as capital adequacy

Q-R2 treats “reserve fraction” and “position sizing” as potentially independent variables. That may be an artifact of practitioner vocabulary.

For a fixed account, “reserve 50%” and “size aggregate exposure to use only 50%” can be economically equivalent.

The deeper research object is:

> **How much capital can be committed to a given exposure while retaining enough uncommitted liquidity/collateral capacity to survive plausible simultaneous loss, volatility, assignment, and margin demands?**

The eventual representation might be reserve fraction, maximum utilization, stress collateral coverage, aggregate exposure limit, or another quantity. Research should not presuppose that “40–50% reserve” is the natural variable.

## 6. Sequential funded-account accounting remains load-bearing

The memo correctly identifies the funded sequential account as a more meaningful economic object than isolated per-trade averages, premium collected, win rate, adjusted cost basis, or arithmetic annualization.

A bounded sequential replication is therefore valuable.

However, Q-R3 risks resurrecting the prior 45/50/21 composite simply because it is already researched. The tested Operating Program must have an operator-relevant reason to matter. The research program should not turn an academically convenient specimen into a de facto canonical program.

The proposed guardrail is good: one preregistered bounded attempt, no parameter search, and either positive or negative outcome retires the question.

## 7. Revision governance is a central Wheelwright problem

The memo identifies a deep unresolved problem:

> How can an operator distinguish “the program is structurally broken” from “this trade/period hurts” before deciding to override, abandon, or revise the program?

Two symmetric failure modes matter:

- **emotional abandonment:** discomfort causes rule changes and destroys process coherence;
- **mechanical self-destruction:** the program is genuinely invalid, but “discipline” becomes justification for continuing it.

Therefore “pick a process and stick to it” is insufficient. A stronger provisional principle is:

> **Precommit both the operating program and the process by which the program itself is reviewed and revised.**

The evidence does not yet supply a validated revision method. A bounded search ending in terminal ABSENCE-OF-EVIDENCE would itself be useful because the operation could then be designed around irreducible uncertainty rather than pretending a validated rule exists.

## 8. Human factors: avoid a behavioral-nanny model

Worker B's pushback is valuable.

The evidence does not justify a simple story in which emotion causes losses and discipline produces survival. Professional traders can exhibit behavioral asymmetries; experience does not necessarily eliminate bias; devices can change behavior without proving improved outcomes; and trading-specific checklist efficacy is unestablished.

The stronger current distinction is:

> psychological effects can perturb decisions; structural constraints can terminate the account.

Human-factors research should therefore prioritize operator-consequential gaps such as revision governance, multi-day loss-conditioned behavior with convex payoffs, and demonstrated survival effects of safeguards—not generic bias catalogs or motivational discipline doctrine.

## 9. Blow-up research: mechanisms, not incidence

The failure-case track is high-value because it identifies mechanisms:

- leverage;
- short convexity/tail exposure;
- insufficient stress capital;
- apparently diversified strategies sharing common downside exposure;
- inability to finance a position through stress;
- accounting that conceals economic loss;
- broker/market structure converting temporary stress into permanent loss;
- automation faithfully executing a bad state;
- exit plans relying on liquidity that disappears when simultaneously demanded.

But the case record is strongly selected toward spectacular, litigated, institutional failures. It cannot establish population incidence for ordinary retail attrition.

Therefore:

> documented cases tell us **ways an operator can fail**, not **how operators usually fail**.

## 10. Q-R1 should not lead with population incidence

The proposed first question—what fraction of short-premium account failures are forced liquidations versus voluntary exits—is conceptually interesting but likely data-starved. The memo already notes that broker account data are not public.

This risks recreating Q0: a legitimate but unavailable denominator consumes research resources.

More operator-relevant and answerable:

> **Under the operating programs and brokerage regimes actually in scope, what conditions can cause the operator to lose disposition authority through collateral or margin constraints, and what observable preconditions exist before that happens?**

This studies the failure boundary rather than requiring an unavailable population prevalence estimate.

The incidence question can remain open without blocking protective understanding of the mechanism.

## 11. Earnings contradiction is valuable only if in scope

The Option Alpha versus InTheMoney earnings contradiction is a good example of practitioner research generating a concrete falsifiable operator question:

> Should an operator intentionally carry the relevant short-premium exposure across an earnings announcement?

It deserves research if intentional single-company earnings exposure is part of the relevant operation. If not, it is domain knowledge rather than a current research priority.

This is the operator-decision test working correctly.

## 12. Compensation-vs-mispricing should remain parked

The memo correctly finds no current change to sizing, reserves, revision governance, measurement, or brokerage capability from adjudicating dealer compensation versus mispricing versus behavioral stickiness.

The temporal-incidence result retains an operator-relevant residue, but its wording should be tightened.

The supported interpretation is not “retail cannot access the premium because markets are closed.” A trader can establish a position before the close and carry it overnight.

The bounded operator conclusion is:

> **The documented negative delta-hedged SPX return appears concentrated in non-trading periods in the inspected historical evidence; harvesting that phenomenon therefore requires bearing exposure across the non-trading interval.**

The economic mechanism remains contested and currently low-priority absent a specific operator decision.

## 13. Macro versus micro should classify consequence, not causal mechanism

Keep the framework, but define it operationally:

- **Macro:** if wrong, the issue can materially threaten the capital base, control of the portfolio, or viability/repeatability of the operating process.
- **Micro:** if wrong, the issue primarily reduces efficiency of an otherwise viable operation.

This avoids treating psychology as inherently macro or structural risk as inherently macro. Evidence must still establish whether the feared consequence actually occurs.

A micro issue can be promoted when evidence shows material survival, repeatability, utilization, or economic impact.

## 14. Research stopping rules are strong and should survive

The memo's stopping/rabbit-hole rules are among its most reusable outputs:

- no operator decision attached → no wave resources;
- terminal absence is declared rather than repeatedly re-dug;
- preregister replications and kill conditions;
- parameter optimization is not automatically a finding;
- falsifier first;
- nulls are results;
- no automatic research-to-policy conversion;
- importance, evidence strength, and actionability remain distinct;
- survivorship accounting accompanies survivor-derived lessons.

“One worker, one question, no cascades” is useful research-process machinery, not domain truth, and should remain at that level.

## 15. Principal-question reconciliation

### Whose operation is modeled?

Avoid the memo's false binary between Principal-specific and generic retail research.

Use:

> **External domain research establishes mechanisms and evidence; the Principal's actual operating conditions determine applicability and priority.**

Principal-specific records should not be generalized into universal options evidence.

### Small-account population

Keep as a domain question unless small-account operation is explicitly in scope. Do not spend current wave resources merely because the practitioner disagreement is interesting.

### Earnings exposure

Requires explicit operator scope before promotion.

### Principal operating records

Treat as **operator-specific observational evidence**. They are useful for funded-account accounting, execution patterns, process adherence, lifecycle behavior, and question generation. They are not external empirical evidence about options traders generally.

### Compensation versus mispricing

Remain parked absent a concrete operator decision.

## 16. Recommended next-wave correction

Do **not** adopt Muse's R1–R5 wave unchanged.

Accept the reorientation, then revise the wave:

1. **Failure boundary / loss of disposition authority** — replace forced-liquidation population incidence with a bounded investigation of the conditions under which relevant structures and broker regimes can remove operator control.
2. **Capital adequacy / stress survivability** — replace reserve-fraction optimization with the deeper relationship among exposure, uncommitted capital, simultaneous stress, volatility, assignment, and margin demands.
3. **Bounded sequential funded-account accounting** — retain, but test an operator-relevant explicitly specified Operating Program rather than resurrecting 45/50/21 by convenience; one preregistered attempt, no parameter search.
4. **Revision governance** — retain as a bounded/timeboxed search; terminal absence is a useful result.
5. **Earnings gap-risk** — promote only if intentional earnings exposure is actually in scope.

Keep parked:

- Q0 precision execution-friction calibration;
- compensation-vs-mispricing mechanism adjudication;
- DTE/IVR/win-rate optimization;
- additional educator-corpus expansion absent a specific missing lineage;
- backtest-of-the-month activity;
- small-account viability unless the relevant operator population is in scope.

## 17. Durable research-program interpretation

The reorientation has moved the program toward the questions Wheelwright actually needs research to inform:

> **What can kill the operation? What can make the operator lose control of it? What can make the operator believe it is working when it is not? What disciplines and capabilities give a real operating process a reasonable chance to survive?**

That is a better priority function than scientific novelty or parameter precision.

It remains a research program, not a trading policy. No finding in the memo or this analysis automatically authorizes a Wheelwright Product requirement, trading rule, implementation, architecture change, or Operating Program.
