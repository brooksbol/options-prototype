## 1. Executive assessment

### The one-paragraph answer

This is **not** an emerging AI trading technology category. It is conventional retail algorithmic trading — deterministic rule engines, statistical pattern-matching, backtesters, and broker API adapters, most of it built years before the current AI wave — **repackaged with AI marketing**, plus a genuinely new but narrow layer: LLM copilots that draft rules, explain charts, and translate plain English into executable if-then logic. That copilot layer is real and useful. It does not trade better. Across 50+ products checked, **not one publishes an audited live track record**, and **not one documents a complete cash-secured-put → assignment → equity ownership → covered-call lifecycle with robust capital accounting and risk management**. The most technically credible products are the ones that say so most plainly. The $20/month "AI will make you money" product is, in every verified case, a deliberately narrowed slice — one tool, spot-only, delayed data, or a broker-subsidised loss-leader — and the full real-time options stack costs $80–$250/month for a structural reason: market-data licensing, not compute. The proliferation is real as a *supply* phenomenon (white-label bot infrastructure, open-source rebrand bases, affiliate/CPA funnels, and crypto's free exchange APIs make a $20 bot cheap to launch), but the headline question — whether ad volume is measurably rising — is **UNKNOWN**: no Meta Ad Library quantitative count was retrievable, and increased visibility through targeting cannot be distinguished from increased supply on the evidence gathered. The market splits into three layers that must not be conflated: (i) real operating tools that sell scanners, backtesters, and automation with backtest or win-rate-only performance and no audited live return; (ii) a white-label / affiliate / forex-CPA supply chain that makes $20–$50/month bots cheap to launch and profitable mainly via shared or free data, local execution, and upsells — not via trading edge; and (iii) a social-media / scam layer (guaranteed returns, non-existent bots, fake dashboards) that regulators have repeatedly charged, most recently SEC Press Release 2026-95 (29 Sep 2026, ≥$15.3m alleged).

### Surprises — findings that contradict the initial hypothesis

1. **The best products make no performance claim at all.** TrendSpider publishes no house return claim; Composer sells a builder and a brokerage, not a strategy; Option Alpha's FAQ states flatly that "there is no way to provide an accurate performance history" for a bot. In this market, the absence of a return claim is a credibility signal, and the presence of a large one is a warning.
2. **The only vendor with a dated live record publishes one that is *worse* than its backtest.** Danelfin (not in the seed list) runs a public audit site: live Score 8–10 alpha −0.5% over three months vs +2.2% backtest, with pre-July-2025 scores labelled "simulated backtest" and the model described as "picked after its performance was known." That transparency is the differentiator, not the return.
3. **Tickeron — one of the most heavily marketed "AI trading robots" — has no confirmed live broker integration.** Its own FAQ (published Nov 2023, updated Jan 2024, still live) states "We don't have any integrations of Robots with clients' brokerage accounts at the moment, but it's under development," roadmapping broker connection for Q3–Q4 2023. As of 2026-11-08 no named broker is confirmed; an independent survey dated 6 Nov 2026 could not confirm that Tickeron places trades in a customer's own account. Marketing describes an autonomous trader; documented architecture describes a signal vendor.
4. **Option Alpha — the closest product to a full options-automation system — is blind to assignment.** Its own order-handling documentation states bots are unaware when an assignment event is not returned through the broker API, and the bot can keep tracking the original pre-assignment spread while the brokerage account now holds shares. The wheel is executed by manually importing the assigned shares. That is the industry's best options-automation UX, and assignment — the pivotal lifecycle event — is a manual step.
5. **A $20 crypto bot and a $20 equities bot are different businesses.** Crypto exchange APIs are free, which is why crypto bots cluster at $20–$29/month. A US-equities vendor charging $20/month cannot buy a $99–$199/month full-SIP feed per user and profit on subscription alone; the viable $20 architectures are shared/amortised feeds, broker-bundled or delayed data, EOD bars only, or running on the user's own PC via a free broker API (vendor compute ≈ $0).
6. **The most serious complaint pattern in the named set is not about returns — it is about withdrawals from a registered broker-dealer.** Composer (Trustpilot 2.7/5, 27 reviews, Dec 2024–May 2026) carries multiple unverified allegations of withdrawal difficulty over two weeks, disappearing conversations, and an inability to unwind a position — against a FINRA-member broker-dealer. Unverified, and not a verdict, but it deserves a BrokerCheck check rather than a Trustpilot verdict.
7. **3Commas killed its v1 platform on 11 Sep 2026 with zero state migration.** Strategies, bots, settings, and API keys did not transfer; previously placed exchange orders remained live on the exchange but unmanaged. That is the single worst architectural failure observed in the study — a money-adjacent system that abandoned state rather than recovering it — and it happened to a platform with a prior ~150,000-API-key credential disclosure (Dec 2022, ~$20–22m estimated losses).
8. **The broker industry drew a line in October 2026, and it is not "bots are banned."** Schwab (Q3 earnings call, 15 Oct 2026) blocks third-party AI agents that log in on clients' behalf; JPMorgan, Bank of America, and Citi were reported the same week taking much the same position. The permitted route is approved API/MCP connections with no money-movement (or per-order approval). Credential-login agents are blocked; API agents are not. That directly affects any vendor relying on brokerage username/password login — the exact practice FINRA warned about on 29 Jul 2025.
9. **Two of the most interesting products were acquired in 2025–2026 and are being absorbed, not expanded.** Capitalise.ai was acquired by Kraken (announced 20 Aug 2025) and its Interactive Brokers connection was terminated 31 Mar 2026 at Kraken's choice; Composer was acquired by SoFi (23 Jun 2026). The independent no-code automation vendor is consolidating into venues, not proliferating as a standalone.
10. **The open-source layer is the real competitive reference, not the $20 SaaS layer.** Freqtrade (56,647 GitHub stars, release 2026.10, FreqAI ML/RL with automatic retraining) and Hummingbot (21,076 stars, v2.17.0, Condor LLM harness connecting "LLM-powered decision-making to deterministic trade execution") disclose more about their decision mechanisms than any commercial vendor in the set — and both are free.

### Conclusions in brief

- **A. Category:** repackaging, with a real but narrow LLM-copilot layer. See §9, A.
- **B. $20/month:** buys a slice (delayed/EOD signals, spot-only crypto, or a broker-subsidised tier). The full real-time options stack is arithmetically implausible at $20 as a standalone subscription with paid acquisition. See §8.
- **C. Credible automation vs signal generator:** custody, broker adapter, order state machine, position reconciliation, risk limits/kill switch, and a reproducible backtest/paper/live chain — none of which a signal generator has. See §9, C and §5.
- **D. Full wheel lifecycle:** no credible retail product verified. Closest are Option Alpha (manual assignment import, no native roll) and ZeroTouch Autopilot (strongest wheel disclosure in the options slice, but ex-div/early-exercise/collateral UNKNOWN). See §9, D and §4.
- **E. AI advantage over deterministic quant:** no demonstrated meaningful predictive advantage. Demonstrated advantages are workflow, scale, and automation — not alpha. See §9, E.
- **F. Trial/benchmark shortlist:** Option Alpha first, Composer second, TrendSpider third, Trade Ideas for signal-quality benchmarking only, 3Commas Starter as a $20-tier architecture reference, Tickeron Real-Time Patterns as a cheap ML-signal benchmark. See §9, F and §9 (engineering opportunities).
- **G. Engineering priorities:** data-freshness/staleness gates; a real S0–S4 state machine with broker reconciliation; an execution-feasibility gate on live bid/ask; a reproducible backtest harness with costs; an ex-div/early-exercise calendar; risk limits + kill switch; the Sentinel reporting hardening; an LLM copilot for rule drafting only, with a structural human-approval boundary; a broker-adapter abstraction; an append-only audit log. See §9.
- **H. Commercial opportunity:** as a standalone $20/month "AI trading" consumer product — unattractive. As a higher-priced research/automation tool, a broker-subsidised tool, or B2B/research licensing — narrowly plausible, but not on the evidence in this slice, and not an objective assumed here. See §9, H.

### 5.2 The robust lifecycle checklist (INFERRED — no verified product meets it)

A product that genuinely handled the full wheel lifecycle would need, at minimum:

1. Broker reconciliation on assignment (position event, not poll-and-hope).
2. Cost basis carried across the transition (put premium → share cost basis → call strike floor).
3. Collateral released / re-pledged automatically on assignment and on call-away.
4. Call strike floored at cost basis (or an explicit, logged override).
5. Early-assignment / ex-div handling (dividend-aware early-exercise logic, ex-div calendar).
6. Per-cycle P&L surviving the transition (one cycle, one P&L, across S0–S4).
7. Kill switch on reconciliation failure (halt, do not trade on a diverged ledger).

**No verified product meets this checklist.** Option Alpha fails (1) and (5) explicitly and (3)/(4) by omission. ZeroTouch Autopilot discloses the wheel loop but (5)/(3)/(6) are UNKNOWN. StockHero's templates document none of it. Composer's manual options document none of it. The MH ecosystem's S0–S4 ontology plus the assignment study's sequential simulator is the only treatment in this investigation that models (1)–(6) as a coherent whole — as research. That is a genuine, defensible advantage, and it is also the reason the MH side should not be presented as a shipping product: the model exists, the product does not.

### 5.3 What is genuinely missing in the MH ecosystem (Slice E)

- Live execution + order state machine (no shipping broker adapter).
- Productised strategy builder / backtester UX (research scripts, not a builder).
- Cloud unattended operation.
- LLM copilot for rule drafting (drafting/explanation only, with a human approval boundary).
- Broker-subsidised distribution (the $0-via-broker model that makes Option Alpha and Composer viable at scale).

### 5.4 Where the MH ecosystem is ahead (Slice E)

- Options microstructure rigour (natural bid/ask fills, spread wedge, break-even spread capture).
- Research reproducibility (frozen protocols, matched dates, block-bootstrap CIs, published negatives, audit corrections before results run).

### 6.3 Shortcomings worth avoiding (28, from Slice D)

1. **Assignment blindness: internal position ledger diverging from the broker ledger**, with manual import/override as the repair. Option Alpha (CLAIMED — vendor order-handling docs). Single largest gap for a wheel/assignment product. Reconcile on broker position events and on every poll; surface divergence as a blocking alert, not a manual chore.
2. **Roll as close-then-reopen via two independent automations sequenced by an event** (not a broker-native one-ticket roll). Option Alpha (CLAIMED — no native roll). Close and re-entry can fill at different times, creating slippage and temporary exposure. A roll must be a single net order (or an explicit two-leg transaction with slippage accounting).
3. **Poll-only evaluation with missed-threshold gaps** (1/5/15-min cadence; thresholds crossed between polls can be missed). Option Alpha (CLAIMED); StockHero minute-scale polling (INFERRED); Composer daily batch (INFERRED). If Sentinel claims to monitor continuously, use streaming quotes or state the poll interval and its miss risk.
4. **Per-bot-only risk; no portfolio-level halt.** No cross-bot exposure cap, account-level daily-loss kill switch, or market-wide halt documented in any product. All 8 — Option Alpha per-bot failsafe is the best documented and still bot-scoped; Capitalise.ai, 3Commas, StockHero, Trade Ideas, TrendSpider, Tickeron, Composer: no portfolio halt evidenced. Bots must not be able to collectively exceed an account-level limit by running independently.
5. **Mid-price trigger evaluation + fee-excluded P&L in backtests.** Option Alpha (VERIFIED — fees excluded; CLAIMED — mid-price triggers); StockHero (slippage excluded, CLAIMED); 3Commas (fee/slippage treatment undisclosed, UNKNOWN). ### 7.4 How to read a vendor performance claim (the taxonomy)

1. **Win rate without average win/loss, sample size, period, or survivorship** is not a performance claim. A 90% win rate can be deeply unprofitable.
2. **Signal price → session high/low** is a highlight reel, not a return. Never report it as a return.
3. **"Odds of Success"** is a historical frequency, not a forward probability, unless n, window, fees, and out-of-sample status are stated.
4. **"Pattern detection accuracy"** without a definition of accuracy is not evaluable.
5. **Backtest without costs** (commissions, fees, slippage, funding, assignment) overstates short-premium economics specifically — the DIA/XSP audit lesson.
6. **Only winning robots / only winning signals released** is survivorship, not selection.
7. **A live record worse than its backtest, published with dates,** is more credible than a backtest presented as live. Danelfin is the model.

### 8.1 The $20/month headline — what it actually buys

$20 is an entry-tier slice, not a full system. Verified leaders price real-time data + automation + AI at $80–$250/mo (Trade Ideas Standard $127/mo / Premium $254, $178 annual; TrendSpider $82–$321/mo; Composer Trading Pass $40/mo, $32 annual; Option Alpha Pro $149/mo, $99 annual, $0 via qualifying broker; Tickeron Patterns $20/mo; 3Commas Starter $20/mo, $15 annual). ### 8.4 What the $20 tier is for (the funnel, stated plainly)

## 9. Engineering opportunities + strategic synthesis

### 9.1 Engineering opportunities, ranked by practical value (Slice E, with Slice D patterns)

1. **Data freshness / staleness gates.** The MH record's own stated concern, and the dimension where competitors are furthest ahead (TrendSpider's named feed catalogue, Option Alpha's live NBBO feed). A staleness gate — refuse to evaluate a signal on data older than X, log the refusal — is cheap, falsifiable, and the single most credible improvement available.
2. **Position / capital state machine with broker reconciliation.** The S0–S4 ontology exists as a model; make it a running state machine that reconciles against the broker on every poll and on every position event, and surfaces divergence as a blocking alert. This is the gap no commercial product has closed — it is the MH ecosystem's clearest opening.
3. **Execution-feasibility gate on live bid/ask.** Before any signal is acted on, check the live bid/ask spread, size, and Greeks freshness; refuse to trade when the spread exceeds a stated threshold. The DIA/XSP audit lesson, productised.
4. **Reproducible backtest harness with costs.** Natural bid/ask fills only, never midpoint as a fill; commissions, fees, slippage, assignment modelled; frozen protocol before data viewed; matched dates; block-bootstrap CIs. The MH record already does this as research — make it a harness others can run.
5. **Ex-div / early-exercise calendar.** Discrete ex-div dates, dividend-aware early-exercise logic, earnings gates. The assignment study's §6 requirements, productised.
6. **Risk limits + kill switch.** Per-bot allocation caps (StockHero/Option Alpha pattern), fatal-vs-soft error taxonomy with per-day auto-off (Option Alpha pattern), **plus** a portfolio-level daily-loss halt and a single kill switch that cancels working orders — which no product documents.
7. **Sentinel reporting hardening.** Every signal carries n, window, definition, and data freshness alongside the point estimate (Tickeron pattern #23, done honestly); backtest limitations stated in the report, not a footnote (Trade Ideas OddsMaker pattern #5); hypothetical-performance concession wording wherever a backtest is shown (Tickeron pattern #27).
8. **LLM copilot for drafting/explanation only, with a human approval boundary.** Plain-English rule authoring with the canonical compiled form shown and confirmed before activation (Capitalise.ai/Composer pattern #20); the LLM never holds the order key (TrendSpider Sidekick pattern #2); any LLM-initiated order passes a **server-side** approval gate (Composer MCP Avoid #2). Drafting and explanation are the demonstrated LLM advantage; live decision-making is not.
9. **Broker adapter abstraction (Tradier / tastytrade / Alpaca first).** Thin per-broker adapters over a common order/position model (Option Alpha pattern), with the adapter interface and its test status published — never a broker name or integration date until the adapter exists and is tested (Tickeron Avoid #17).
10. **Append-only monitoring / audit log.** Bot Log recording decision inputs for every run, including runs that prevented a trade (Option Alpha pattern #13); alert-delivery semantics (at-least-once vs exactly-once) and missed-alert behaviour on disconnect published; a status/incident log — precisely because incumbents do not publish one (Avoid #26).

**Deprioritise:** a proprietary ML signal model (no demonstrated advantage, §9 E); a $20 consumer subscription (unit economics, §8); "AI" branding (the label is the liability, §7).

### 9.2 Strategic synthesis — questions A–H, in the brief's order

**A. Is "AI trading" a real emerging technology category, or repackaging?**

**Repackaging, with a real but narrow new layer.** The products are, in the main, conventional retail algorithmic trading — deterministic rule engines, statistical pattern-matching, backtesters, broker API adapters — most of it built years before the current AI wave, now relabelled. The genuinely new layer is narrow and specific: (i) LLM copilots that draft rules, explain charts, and translate plain English into executable if-then logic (TrendSpider Sidekick, Composer AI draft, Capitalise.ai NLP compiler, 3Commas AI Assistant); (ii) LLM access layers (MCP servers) shipped 2025–2026 by TradingView, QuantConnect, Alpaca, TradeStation, NinjaTrader; (iii) user-trained classical ML (TrendSpider Quant Lab: Random Forest, KNN, Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression) with out-of-sample discipline; (iv) nightly statistical selection (Trade Ideas Holly). None of these is a demonstrated predictive advantage. The category that is genuinely emerging is *LLM-assisted rule authoring and workflow automation* — not *AI that trades better*. The marketing that claims the second is repackaging; the products that claim the first are, for the most part, telling the truth.

**B. What does $20/month actually buy, and is a full system plausible at that price?**

**A slice, not a system — and a full real-time options system is not plausible at $20 as a standalone subscription.** $20 buys: one tool (Tickeron Real-Time Patterns), spot-only crypto automation (3Commas Starter), a delayed/EOD signal feed, a broker-subsidised tier, or a research screener (StockSmith.ai, Kavout Pro). The full stack — real-time SIP + OPRA, live options automation, LLM agent — costs $80–$250/month at verified leaders for a structural reason: market-data licensing (OPRA $1,500/mo flat + $1.25/mo per non-pro subscriber; Massive $199/mo per asset class; ThetaData $40–$160 personal; Alpaca Algo Trader Plus $99/mo = full SIP + real-time OPRA), not compute (LLM daily summary ~$0.007–0.009, agentic loop ~$0.10–$1.00/session). The archetype model (INFERRED, 1,000 subs): delayed/EOD signals viable (~$13–17 contribution); real-time equity marginal (~$10–14); real-time options automation with vendor-redistributed OPRA + LLM agent NOT viable standalone at $20 (~−$5 to +$8 at 1,000 subs, deeply negative below ~300). CAC above ~$150–200 unprofitable at $20 at 3–7%/mo churn. The $20 tier is a funnel; that funnel is the business model — upsells, marketplace rentals, add-ons, or broker economics. A $20 crypto bot is a different business from a $20 equities bot because crypto exchange APIs are free.

**C. How do we distinguish a credible automation product from a signal generator / marketing front?**

**By custody, adapter, state machine, reconciliation, risk, and reproducibility — none of which a signal generator has.** A credible automation product: (1) names its broker(s) and shows a working adapter (not a roadmap); (2) discloses its order state machine (submit → fill report → timeout/cancel/retry, partial-fill handling); (3) reconciles its internal position ledger against the broker and surfaces divergence; (4) documents risk limits and a kill switch (per-bot *and* portfolio-level); (5) publishes a reproducible backtest/paper/live chain with costs, slippage, and fill model stated; (6) names its data feed, frequency, and freshness; (7) states what its "AI" actually is (deterministic rules / statistical model / LLM translation / named ML) with the model, split, and out-of-sample status. A signal generator has none of these — it has a score, a win rate, and a manual copy step. A marketing front has the score and the win rate without even the manual copy step being honest about what happens next. Tickeron is the clearest test case: a signal vendor marketed as an autonomous trader, whose own FAQ says no brokerage integration exists. Option Alpha is the clearest positive test case: it documents its assignment blindness, its inability to backtest a bot, and its Schwab reauth failure mode — and is more credible for it.

**D. Which products genuinely handle the full wheel lifecycle (CSP → assignment → stock → covered call), with robust capital accounting?**

**None verified.** Closest, in order: (1) **Option Alpha** — the only product with a documented, working options-automation lifecycle, but assignment-blind (bots unaware when an assignment event is not returned through the broker API; bot can keep tracking the original spread while the account holds shares), no native roll (close-then-reopen via two automations), manual wheel import (user must manually import assigned shares; spreads resulting in assignment must be manually overridden to reflect full premium retained), no dividend-aware early-exercise logic documented. (2) **ZeroTouch Autopilot** — strongest wheel disclosure in the whole study (VERIFIED: "On assignment it closes and sells or buys back only the shares it produced, or continues the wheel," wheel defaults 0.30Δ, 30–45d), but ex-div/early-exercise/collateral UNKNOWN, IBKR only, $299/$499/$999/mo by rule count, and the bot does nothing while the PC is off. (3) **OptionsWheel.net** — full wheel CLAIMED, broker UNKNOWN, $49/mo. (4) **StockHero** — Wheel and Iron Condor templates listed (VERIFIED as a listed feature), but **zero lifecycle documentation found** — no strike/delta/DTE selection, no assignment/early-exercise/expiration/collateral/rolling/leg-atomicity documentation. It proves retail demand for no-code wheel automation and nothing about how to do it correctly. The robust checklist (§5.2: broker reconciliation on assignment, cost basis carried across transition, collateral released/re-pledged automatically, call strike floored at cost basis, early-assignment/ex-div handling, per-cycle P&L surviving transition, kill switch on reconciliation failure) is met by **no verified product**. The MH ecosystem's S0–S4 ontology plus the assignment study's sequential simulator is the only coherent treatment found — as research, not as a shipping product.

**E. What is the actual AI advantage over a deterministic quant system, if any?**

**No demonstrated meaningful predictive advantage from any competitor.** Credible AI in this market is: Holly's nightly backtest selection (statistical, not predictive — it re-ranks rules, it does not forecast); Tickeron's pattern standardisation (empirical base rates, not a calibrated forecast); LLM copiloting (TrendSpider Sidekick — "never places trades by itself," "cannot judge trendline quality"; Composer AI drafting); NLP→rules compilation (Capitalise.ai). No audited, live, cost-inclusive return beating a stated deterministic benchmark exists for any vendor in the set. The demonstrated advantages are **workflow, scale, and automation** — drafting rules faster, scanning more symbols, running unattended, translating plain English into if-then logic — not alpha. The honest statement of the AI advantage is: it lowers the cost of *expressing* a strategy and *operating* it, and it does not, on any evidence found, improve the strategy's expectancy. Any MH ML feature should be held to the TrendSpider Quant Lab standard: name the model, state the split, require out-of-sample before live, test across markets/timeframes — and expect it to fail that test more often than not.

**F. Which products are worth trialling / benchmarking against, and which should be ignored?**

Trial, in order: **(1) Option Alpha** — first. Only options-native automation with a real broker path and honest constraints; trial the assignment blind spot deliberately. **(2) Composer** — second. Best rule-builder UX, cleanest AI-boundary doctrine, broker-dealer status as the distribution reference. **(3) TrendSpider** — third. Best ML disclosure and strongest LLM approval boundary; benchmark signal quality, not options trading. **(4) Trade Ideas Premium** — benchmark only. Holly is the only long-running nightly-selection system with any independent tracking. **(5) 3Commas Starter** — $20-tier architecture reference. Study how a $20 crypto bot is built and why that architecture does not transfer to US options. **(6) Tickeron Real-Time Patterns** — cheap ML-signal benchmark ($20/mo). Test pattern base rates, not trading. **Do not trial:** StockHero (options templates least-documented part of a thin automation layer), Capitalise.ai (sunsetting surface; IBKR already terminated), any $20 "passive income" product that cannot name its broker, its data feed, and its backtest methodology. The trial protocol, if run, should be: paper first (30–60 days, Option Alpha's own recommendation), signal-only mode where available, natural bid/ask fills only, costs included, and the assignment blind spot tested deliberately — not a backtest comparison.

**G. What are the highest-value engineering patterns to adopt, and which shortcomings to avoid?**

Adopt, in priority order: data-freshness/staleness gates; a real S0–S4 state machine with broker reconciliation; an execution-feasibility gate on live bid/ask; a reproducible backtest harness with costs (natural bid/ask fills only, never midpoint as a fill); an ex-div/early-exercise calendar; risk limits + kill switch (per-bot *and* portfolio-level); Sentinel reporting hardening (n, window, definition, freshness on every signal); an LLM copilot for drafting/explanation only with a structural human-approval boundary; a broker-adapter abstraction (Tradier/tastytrade/Alpaca first); an append-only audit log. The full 30-pattern list is in §6.2; the ranked engineering priorities are in §9.1. Avoid, in priority order: assignment blindness (the single largest gap — reconcile on broker position events and on every poll; surface divergence as a blocking alert); roll as close-then-reopen (a roll must be a single net order or an explicit two-leg transaction with slippage accounting); poll-only evaluation with missed-threshold gaps (state the poll interval and its miss risk, or use streaming quotes); per-bot-only risk (bots must not collectively exceed an account-level limit); mid-price triggers + fee-excluded P&L in backtests (always report natural bid/ask fills and net-of-fees P&L alongside any mid reference — the DIA/XSP audit lesson); backtest/live parity asserted, never demonstrated (publish the backtest's exact assumptions alongside every result); win rate as a headline metric (headline expectancy with confidence intervals, or nothing); "profit potential" (signal price → session high/low) as a return; "Odds of Success" as a forward probability without n, window, and out-of-sample status; "AI" as a label for deterministic rules (name the decision mechanism exactly); MCP approval in the host app, not server-side (any LLM-initiated order must pass a server-side approval gate); state that cannot be exported and rebuilt (versioned, exportable strategy/position state and a documented migration path from day one — the 3Commas v1 lesson); credential custody as an afterthought; roadmapping an integration before the adapter exists; webhook-bridge opacity; undisclosed broker-adapter failure modes; options templates with zero lifecycle documentation; silent reauth failure that leaves open positions unmanaged; pricing maze / stale documentation drift. The full 28-item list is in §6.3.

**H. Is there a commercial opportunity here, and in what shape?**

**As a standalone $20/month "AI trading" consumer product: no — unattractive, on this evidence.** Unit economics (real-time options automation not viable standalone at $20; CAC above ~$150–200 unprofitable at $20 at 3–7%/mo churn), SEC AI-washing exposure (Delphia $225k + Global Predictions $175k Mar 2024, Rimar Oct 2024, Presto Jan 2025, Nate Apr 2025 — the SEC has charged "AI" claims that were not substantiated, and the 29 Sep 2026 Cryptoaiml/TSAI charges allege no AI bots existed at all), customer expectations (a $20 product that promises "AI will make you money" creates an expectation no backtest can meet), and no demonstrated alpha (no vendor in the set has an audited live return to sell). Least-bad shapes, **if ever considered — separate decision, not recommended on this evidence:** (i) a $99–149/mo research/automation tool with **no return claims** (the Option Alpha / TrendSpider price point, selling tooling and honesty, not alpha); (ii) a broker-subsidised tool (the Option Alpha $0-via-broker / Composer model — the broker pays, the user pays nothing, the vendor is not selling a return); (iii) B2B/research licensing (the tooling, the backtest harness, the lifecycle model — licensed, not sold as a consumer return). All three require legal review first, and none should be pursued on the strength of this study alone — this study is research, not a business plan, and it is not promoted into Wheelwright authority. The most defensible commercial position in this market, on the evidence, is the one the best vendors already occupy: sell a tool, disclose its limits, make no return claim, and let the user's own testing decide.

### 9.3 Final strategic judgment

The retail AI trading market, as of 2026-11-08, is a **tool market wearing a return market's clothes**. The tools are real — some of them good, a few of them excellent — and the returns are, in every verified case, unproven. The $20/month promise is a funnel, not a system. The AI is, in the main, a copilot, not a trader. The options lifecycle — the thing Wheelwright is built to get right — is the thing no commercial product has gotten right, including the best one, which is honest enough to say so. That is the opportunity, and it is not a commercial opportunity in the $20 consumer sense. It is an engineering opportunity: build the state machine, the reconciliation loop, the execution gate, the reproducible harness, and the audit log that no vendor has built, and do it in the open, with the same protocol discipline — frozen before data viewed, matched dates, block-bootstrap CIs, negatives published — that the MH record already demonstrates. The market's best products will not be beaten on marketing; they will be beaten, if they are beaten at all, on honesty about what is and is not known, and on the one lifecycle no one has yet closed. Until that lifecycle is closed in a shipping product with a broker adapter and a reconciliation loop, the correct statement of the MH ecosystem's position is: **ahead as a model, behind as a product, and the gap is the work.**
