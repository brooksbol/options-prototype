Historical research per symbol via web: dated "most active ETF options" rankings, unusual-activity records, news/forum threads, OCC information memos, SEC filings, and issuer pages. A dedicated crosswalk searched for annual per-ETF options-volume rankings for 2018–2025.

**Anchor fact:** none of the 84 symbols appears in the CBOE 2026 YTD top-100 ETF options-volume table. In the table below, `vol=0` means "absent from that top-100 list," not zero volume — several symbols have independently measured nonzero volume (see column 3).

**Confidence key:** High = hard dated volume/OI figures or official memos; Medium = dated third-party snapshots or consistently negative search results across many sources; Low = current snapshot only, or pure absence-of-evidence for the historical claim. "Indeterminate" is a finding, not a gap in effort — for most of these symbols no dated historical options record exists anywhere on the public web.

## 2) Aggregate observations

**1. The cohort is uniformly thin today, with one genuine exception.** 83 of 84 symbols sit outside the CBOE 2026 top-100 with 1–5 listed expirations (monthly only) and little or no measured open interest. The exception is **LABD** (Direxion Daily S&P Biotech Bear 3X): 12 expirations including weeklies, ~3,000 contracts/day (30d avg), ~28–46k total OI, OI at ~89th percentile of its own trailing year. It is the only member with a functioning retail-scale options market.

**2. Today's thinness is representative of the historical record for ~75 of 84 symbols.** Thorough searches found no dated evidence that the options markets of the other symbols were ever materially more usable. "Indeterminate" dominates the historical column — but that is itself informative: across 2010–2026 windows, none of these funds' options ever generated enough activity to appear in a most-active ranking, an unusual-activity story, or an indexed trading thread.

**3. Four cases where the current snapshot materially understates past peaks — the misleading-snapshot risk runs here:**
- **EUO:** genuinely active options market in the 2010–2011 euro crisis (4,299 + 2,182 calls in one day, 7x average, 118:1 call/put). Today's ~35/day is a shadow. Classification: deteriorating.
- **DJP:** episodic ~2,000–2,300-contract unusual-activity days in Mar 2022 and Apr 2023 (commodity boom + Barclays' ETN issuance suspension). Today's chain is effectively dead. Classification: episodic.
- **SLX:** a ~3,750-contract institutional ratio spread on 2010-05-11; a 372-call day as recently as Aug 2026. Routine volume is near zero. Classification: episodic.
- **RETL:** OCC memo evidence of a full high-strike options book when the underlying traded ~$155 in the 2020–2021 meme era; today's $7.11 underlying with single-digit strikes is the decayed remnant of a 3x leveraged product. Classification: episodic/deteriorating.

**4. The snapshot's `vol=0` understates measurable current activity in the other direction, too.** "Absent from the CBOE top-100" is a high bar. Independently measured nonzero markets include: TLH (~120/day, ~3,900 OI), VOT (~75–100/day, ~1,500–1,800 OI), VTIP (~108/day, ~3,100 OI), EWQ (~65/day, ~4,950 OI), EFV (~9/day, ~14,600 OI), GSG (~40/day, ~2,600 OI), MORT (~90/day, ~2–3.4k OI), ASHS (~45/day, ~3–4k OI). None approaches top-100 scale, but none is zero either.

**5. Share liquidity does not imply options liquidity — the starkest pattern in the cohort.** EFV (~$29B AUM, ~2M shares/day) has ~9 contracts/day. USMV (~$23.6B) has ~16/day. SCHF (~$68–69B AUM) prints 5–189/day. SPDW (~$41B) prints ~6/day. VGSH (~$29–35B) prints ~35/day. Conversely, ASHS (~$34M AUM) carries ~3–4k OI and BIZD printed a 12,871-put day. AUM and share volume are not proxies for options liquidity in this cohort.

**6. Long listing history does not imply a liquid past.** iShares options were listed by the early/mid-2000s (IWB: SEC Amex fee filing 6/9/2006; Russell-fund options by 2003) and 20-year-old names like IWB, IJS, IJT, IEV, IOO, IUSG, EXI, EZU, FBT, QTEC have no dated evidence of ever being meaningfully active. The Q1 2011 OCC top-ETF-options list (SPY, IWM, QQQ, EEM, XLF) confirms the concentration was already extreme 15 years ago.

**7. Fund-structure changes that break historical continuity.** Ticker renames: SPDW (GWL→SPDW, 10/2017), XNTK (MTK→XNTK, 9/2017, plus index change), IDGT (IGN→IDGT, 12/2023, plus index switch), OPPJ (DXJS→OPPJ, 7/2025–2026, plus strategy shift). Benchmark changes: SPTM and SPSM (1/2020), VOT (2013; Morningstar rename 7/2026), LTL (2019 telecom→communication services), EURL (2016 index), VGSH (2017), TLH (2016), IUSG (2017 Russell→S&P), QQEW (12/2025 restructure). ETN events: DJP (Barclays issuance suspension 4/2022–7/2022). Reverse splits: LABD (2020, 2025), RETL (five splits 2013–2021). Pre-change options history for these symbols is effectively unrecoverable from public sources — a structural reason some "pasts" will stay indeterminate.

**8. Issuer/vendor labeling conflicts — a current-state (not historical) mislead risk.** SSGA's own fund pages say "Options Available: No" for MMTM, SPYV, XNTK, XTN, and HYMB, while live aggregators (Barchart, Nasdaq, finviz) show listed chains. MarketBeat lists NFTY, EXI, and CSD as "Optionable: N/A" while chains exist. Finviz's VONG chain page says options are "not available" while displaying an all-zero chain. These are vendor/issuer labeling quirks, not delisting signals — but a snapshot built on issuer flags alone would wrongly classify these as non-optionable.

**9. Snapshot-design caveat.** The study's `oi4` (open interest across the first four expirations) understates total OI when interest concentrates in longer-dated expiries — most visible in LABD (oi4=3,928 vs ~28–46k total OI) and visible in VOT. Third-party total-OI figures (MarketChameleon, Barchart) should supplement oi4 when classifying persistence.

**10. Trajectory tally (best estimates, overlapping labels):** persistent-thin ~45, indeterminate ~30, episodic ~7 (DJP, SLX, BIZD, RETL, SPSM, SPTM, SPYV), mildly improving 1–2 (QQQE, SPSM), deteriorating 2–3 (EUO, ROBT mild, MMTM flagged for possible delisting). No symbol supports a "broadly improving" classification. Confidence is medium for current-state facts, low for most historical claims — the two exceptions being EUO (medium-high) and DJP (medium).

**11. Evidence-quality caveat (methodological).** The public web holds almost no dated historical options volume/OI data for non-top-tier ETF options. No annual per-ETF options-volume ranking for 2018–2025 was found in any year; Barchart's per-symbol options history goes back to Oct 2018 but is paywalled; MarketChameleon's daily-history pages are JS-gated. The highest-value untried step is pulling Wayback Machine snapshots of Barchart's ETF options volume-leader pages at each 12/31 from 2018–2025 and checking cohort membership. Everything labeled "indeterminate" above is absence-of-evidence after a real search, not a shrug — but absence-of-evidence is not evidence of absence.
